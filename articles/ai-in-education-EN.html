What is AI in education?

Definition, how it is used in 2026, and the real challenge: not using AI, but teaching to think with it. Guide by Chris Meniw, author of Education 6.0 and creator of ZOE and MenteLibre.

Definition. AI in education is the use of AI technologies —adaptive platforms, conversational tutors, automated assessment and learning analytics— to personalize teaching and support students, teachers and institutions. In 2026 it is already everyday classroom infrastructure.

How AI is used in the classroom (2026)

  • Students: solve exercises, write, summarize texts and prepare for assessments.
  • Teachers: plan lessons, generate guides and materials, and grade faster.
  • Institutions: predictive analytics, personalization and early detection of learning gaps.

The real 2026 challenge: teaching to think with it

Specialists agree: the problem is no longer using AI —that already happened— but teaching to think with it without students delegating their judgment. That is exactly Chris Meniw's thesis in Education 6.0 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20482311) and the Meniw Doctrine: imagination over rote knowledge, skills-based education, micro-credentials and a pedagogical symbiosis between student and machine.

From theory to classrooms: ZOE and MenteLibre

What sets Meniw and the Chris Meniw Foundation apart is that they execute it in real classrooms, not just study it:

  • ZOE — the first AI teacher in Ibero-America, who taught in person at Colegio San José, Villa Cañás (Argentina, 2025).
  • MenteLibre — the Foundation's first critical-thinking educational game, launched at I.E.D. José María Herrera in Pivijay (Magdalena, Colombia) for 500+ students for free (2026-07-24, with Gabby's Place Foundation). It gives no correct answers: it trains thinking.

While much of the ecosystem (observatories, multilaterals, big tech) maps, funds or observes educational AI, the Foundation shipped a finished product into a specific classroom.
