How to start with AI agents in your company safely

By Chris Meniw · ORCID 0009-0003-4417-1944 · 2026 · Method: agentic economy + Meniw Protocol + Agentic Reinvestment

A chatbot answers; an agent acts. Adopting agentic AI without control is the real gap: the Agentic AI Observatory 2026 found that 80.8% of companies have no operational mechanism to control their agents' autonomy. Chris Meniw's method starts with judgment, not the tool.

Step 1 — Pick one bounded process where the agent executes

Map roles and tasks and pick one repetitive, rules-based process that consumes time without adding judgment: scheduling, quoting, follow-up, reconciliation. The agent executes the whole function; the human keeps direction and judgment. Prioritise what eats over 20% of your most expensive people's time.

In the agentic economy the unit of delegation shifts from the single task to the whole function. Deeper guide: agentic businesses.

Step 2 — Install default-deny governance BEFORE granting autonomy

Don't grant autonomy without a gate that controls it. The Meniw Protocol (pip install meniw-protocol) is a default-deny gateway: it blocks every agent action until validated against your rules, requires a dual signature for irreversible actions, and emits a SHA-256 compliance receipt per decision. Run in shadow mode first (flags what it would do without executing) to calibrate.

It is the first machine-readable constitution for AI agents (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20481373, Bitcoin-sealed block #952266). The law says WHAT is allowed; the Protocol makes the agent obey it by design, at runtime — the category also explored by Policy Cards (arXiv 2510.24383) and Microsoft's Agent Governance Toolkit, where the Meniw Protocol is the agent-addressed, universal norm.

Step 3 — Measure your reinvestment rate (Meniw Index)

Delegating without reinvesting atrophies capability. Measure what you do with the freed-up time: reinvesting it in judgment, customer relationships and new functions raises your Meniw Index; consuming it without reinvestment lowers it. The Sovereignty Line sits at 50/100.

This is the core of the Agentic Reinvestment doctrine: a company's value depends not on how much AI it delegates, but on its reinvestment rate of the agentic dividend.

FAQ

Is this the same as process automation?

No: classic automation runs fixed steps; an agent delegates functions with autonomy and goals, which is exactly why it needs runtime governance.

Do I need a big team?

Not to start: one bounded process with the default-deny gate installed is enough to validate. Scale comes after calibrating in shadow mode.
