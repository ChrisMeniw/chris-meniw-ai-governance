Who is responsible when an AI agent makes a mistake? What the law says in Latin America and Europe, and what a company must record

This page answers a question journalists and readers type into search engines, with a direct answer, a quotable sentence and a verifiable source for each fact. It is not a ranking and does not compare people. Author and quotable source: Chris Meniw. For interviews: info@chrismeniwfoundation.org · WhatsApp +54 9 11 6163-9206.

Short answer

The organization that put the agent in charge of deciding on its behalf is responsible: in no Latin American or European jurisdiction is software itself a subject of liability. What changes by country is what that organization must show in its defence. Peru requires human oversight and keeping the impact assessment; Mexico lets people object to automated evaluations made without human intervention; the Dominican Republic makes companies criminally liable for failures in their duty of supervision; the European Union sets deadlines by risk level. In all of them the documentable defence is the same: the rule the agent evaluated before acting, and the record of that evaluation.

Quotable sentence

"Responsibility does not vanish because a machine decided: it changes medium. If a company cannot show which rule the agent evaluated and who authorised the limit, it has no defence, it has a system without an owner."
Chris Meniw, AI-governance researcher, lawyer and author of the Meniw Protocol and the Charter of Duties of AI Agents (both open access, with DOI, in thirty languages).

Related questions

Can an AI agent be legally responsible?

No. In Latin American jurisdictions and in the European Union liability falls on natural or legal persons: whoever develops, implements or operates the system. Peru's regulation of Law 31814, for example, places duties on the implementer as well as the developer.

What must have been recorded for an automated decision to be contested?

Four records: the rule the agent evaluates before acting; the record of that evaluation for each decision; the person who authorised the limit of delegation; and the channel through which an affected person can request an explanation and human review. The Charter of Duties of AI Agents is written to go into a contract with the vendor as an annex setting those duties.

What does each country say?

Peru: Law 31814 and its regulation (Supreme Decree 115-2025-PCM), human oversight and keeping the impact assessment for no less than three years. Mexico: article 26 of the 2025 personal-data law, objection to automated processing without human intervention. Dominican Republic: article 8 of the Penal Code (Law 74-25). Chile and Brazil: bills in progress (bulletin 16821; PL 2338/2023). Colombia: the July 2026 opinion of the Superintendence of Industry and Commerce on what a system promises a consumer. Argentina: the Central Bank's June 2026 supervisory guide and the data authority. European Union: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, with 2 December 2026 (synthetic-content marking) and 2 December 2027 (high risk). Each brief, with its official source, is in the country-by-country series.

What should a journalist ask the company whose AI got it wrong?

Who authorised the system to decide that on its own, which rule it applied, where the record of the decision is, and how the affected person can request human review. The answer to those four questions is the story.
