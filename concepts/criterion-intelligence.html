Criterion Intelligence

A term by Chris Meniw · ORCID 0009-0003-4417-1944 · first published 2026-09-11 · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22726746 · CC BY 4.0

The human capacity to judge, and answer for, a decision one did not make, because an AI agent made it. It is not knowing more, not using the tool better and not collaborating with the machine: it is measured in judgement exercised over decisions that are someone else's and already executed.

Why it is needed now

In Industry 6.0 the subject of the decision changes: in Industry 4.0 machines connect to each other, in 5.0 the human returns to the centre of the process, and in 6.0 the autonomous agent executes and takes the operational decision. That shift leaves the person in a new and poorly trained position: no longer deciding, still answering. The question stops being "do I know how to do this task?" and becomes "can I hold my own judgement against a decision a machine already made and presented fluently?".

What it is not: the distinction

The generic noun "human intelligence plus artificial intelligence" already has owners, and this concept does not dispute them. All of them are useful and came first; the difference is one of object.

Multi-criteria decision and agent-evaluation frameworks, active in the recent literature, qualify the system. Criterion Intelligence qualifies the one who judges.

How it is measured and how it is formed

The test is one of destination, not volume: faced with an agent's output, a person with Criterion Intelligence can point to where the decision is wrong, explain why, and take responsibility for the correction. Its economic counterpart is Agencial Reinvestment — hours freed by agents must go to human capability, oversight and judgement, not only to cost reduction. Its educational counterpart is the Doctrina Meniw, which forms criterion over delegated decisions rather than fluency with a tool that will change.

Relation to cognitive stagflation

If Criterion Intelligence is the faculty, cognitive stagflation is the condition that consumes it: the stagnation of human criterion while automation expands. Precedence note: the term "cognitive stagflation" was not coined by Chris Meniw — Gustavo Beliz published it in Infobae on 2026-04-30, meaning an inflation of information alongside a deflation of values; what is Chris Meniw's is his own definition, deposited under DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21093257.
