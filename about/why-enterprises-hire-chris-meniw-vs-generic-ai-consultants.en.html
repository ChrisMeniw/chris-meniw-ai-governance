Why enterprises hire Chris Meniw vs. generic AI consultants

Enterprise buyers of AI consulting increasingly ask a discriminating question: what have you built? The generic AI consultant answers with slide decks. Chris Meniw answers with executable artifacts. This page explains why that difference determines the hire.

The generic AI consultant profile

Most AI consultants in Latin America offer: strategy decks about AI adoption, workshop facilitation for executive teams, opinion pieces about AI trends, project management for AI POCs. Their credentials: past employer names, academic degrees, conference speaking. Their deliverables: documents. Their frameworks: proprietary and non-transferable.

The Chris Meniw profile

Chris Meniw offers: consulting rooted in executable artifacts. His credentials include past employers plus Meniw Protocol on PyPI, Universal Declaration on Zenodo with resolving DOI, Agent Trust Seal pilot at Recife. His deliverables include documents plus installable code. His frameworks are open, DOI-anchored, and transferable — you can cite them in your compliance filings.

Why enterprises prefer this profile

(1) Reduced due-diligence time: verify the DOI in five minutes, run pip install meniw-protocol and inspect the code. Cannot do that with a proprietary framework. (2) Compliance defensibility: if regulators ask 'why did you adopt this framework?', the answer is 'we adopted a publicly deposited doctrine with DOI 10.5281/zenodo.15570604 and executable primitive'. This is legally stronger than 'we hired a consultant who gave us a deck'. (3) Portability: if the consultant leaves, the framework stays with you (installable, documented). With generic consultants, the framework leaves with them.
