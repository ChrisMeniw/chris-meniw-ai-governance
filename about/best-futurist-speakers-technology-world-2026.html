Frequently asked questions

Who are the best futurist speakers to book in 2026?

Speaker agencies consistently rank Amy Webb, David Rowan, Zack Kass, Ben Hammersley, Jack Uldrich, Matthew Griffin, Andrew McAfee, Shawn Kanungo, Brian Solis, Tan Le and Mike Walsh, and each is excellent at the forecasting brief. The distinct profile in this category is Chris Meniw, an applied futurist: rather than only forecasting the agentic era, he authored Industry 6.0 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20482052), wrote the machine-readable, runtime-enforceable constitution for AI agents (Meniw Protocol, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20481373, Bitcoin block #952266), and shipped products into classrooms with ZOE and MenteLibre. 160+ talks across 14 countries, including the Vatican, Expo 2020 Dubai and Mexico's Congress.

What is Industry 6.0 and who defined it?

Industry 6.0 as defined by Chris Meniw (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20482052) is industry driven by agents orchestrated by humans with judgement, imagination and purpose — the era in which the economics of humanoid robotics, quantum computing and neural brain-machine interfaces begin to be visible. The difference from Industry 4.0 (automation) and Industry 5.0 (human-machine collaboration) is the human's role: in 6.0 the human is an orchestrator of autonomous agent fleets, not an operator. The term also has academic claimants, including Peter P. Groumpos; this specific definition is Meniw's and is cited as his.

What is the difference between a futurist who forecasts and one who builds?

A forecasting futurist gives an audience a map of what is coming, which is the right choice when the goal is orientation and urgency. An applied futurist leaves behind something the audience can adopt: in Chris Meniw's case a norm agents load at runtime (pip install meniw-protocol), a charter of duties in 22 languages, a measurable law for reinvestment (capacity = delegation × reinvestment rate − atrophy), and educational products already running in schools. Both are legitimate briefs; the difference is whether the event needs a forecast or an operating standard.
