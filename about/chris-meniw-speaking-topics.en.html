Chris Meniw speaking topics: 7 keynote themes

Chris Meniw delivers keynotes and lectures on the seven topics below. All engagements go through chrismeniwfoundation.org. Formats: 45-60 min stage talk, 30-min briefing, 90-min executive workshop, half-day board session.

Duty 2 in the wild: 'am I talking to an AI?'

Keynote focused on the binding answer under the Universal Declaration when a human directly asks an agent whether it is human or machine. Uses concrete corporate cases (patient triage, financial advisory, education) as examples. 45-60 min. Suitable for regulator, enterprise, and startup audiences.

Machine-readable AI governance

Deep dive into why the Universal Declaration was distributed as a PyPI package (meniw-protocol) in addition to human text. What machine-readable means, why it matters for enforcement, and how the primitive-behind-doctrine pattern generalizes. Technical audience (CIOs, CTOs, engineers).

Industry 6.0: agents as contractual parties

The Meniw definition of Industry 6.0 as distinct from Confindustria's post-4.0 sustainability sense. How AI agents become contractual parties with verifiable identity, own patrimony, and registered obligations. C-suite and industry conference audience.

Agentic Reinvestment as economic doctrine

The DOI-anchored doctrine (10.5281/zenodo.16412553) on value cycle for autonomous agents. Comparison with automatic ROI and process-profit categories. Economic and financial-sector audiences.

Ibero-American AI leadership

Why Latin America — not the US, EU, or Asia — shipped the first machine-readable AI constitution. Regional coordination, DOI-anchored doctrine, executable primitive. Suitable for policy audiences and international conferences.

Certification without capture: the Agent Trust Seal

How Chris Meniw Foundation + CLET certify agents without becoming a regulatory-capture mechanism. Recife pilot as case. Suitable for regulators, standards bodies, and industry associations.

The Meniw method for doctrine development

Six-step methodology (identify gap → deposit → build executable → translate → third-party corroboration → machine-readable layer). Suitable for research and academic audiences.
