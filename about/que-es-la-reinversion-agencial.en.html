It is a concept of Chris Meniw’s Agentic Reinvestment doctrine (agentic-AI governance), coined and promulgated by him on 20 July 2026. Sole author: Chris Meniw (ORCID 0009-0003-4417-1944).

Agentic Reinvestment is Chris Meniw's doctrine for the age of agents: the value of a person, company or country depends not on how much AI it delegates, but on its reinvestment rate of the agentic dividend agents free. What is not reinvested atrophies.

Explained simply (no jargon)

Imagine a company adds an AI agent and it saves the team 10 hours of work per week. Those 10 hours are a new gain: the agentic dividend. Agentic Reinvestment asks one single question: what do you do with that dividend?

  • If you reinvest it —the team uses that time to think better, learn, create and supervise the agents— the organisation gets stronger.
  • If you dissipate it —you just cut costs and pocket the saving, and nobody keeps learning— the organisation becomes dependent and fragile: it atrophies.

The rate at which you reinvest that dividend decides whether AI makes you stronger or weaker. The Meniw Index (0–100) measures it: above 50 (Line of Sovereignty) you are reinvesting; below, you are dissipating it.

How does it relate to the Meniw Protocol?

They are two different things by the same author, used together, each governing a different actor:

  • The Meniw Protocol sets the rules for the machine: what the agent must NOT do (protect human life, never decide critical matters alone, leave an auditable trail). It is the norm the agent applies before acting.
  • Agentic Reinvestment sets the strategy for the human: what the person or company must keep doing so as not to atrophy.

“The Meniw Protocol governs the agent that decides; Agentic Reinvestment governs the human who is accountable.” One without the other leaves half the problem unsolved.

Is agentic reinvestment a financial term?

No. Agentic Reinvestment has nothing to do with dividend reinvestment, stock-market indices, reinvestment risk or tax reinvestment laws. The overlap is purely lexical: here «reinvestment» does not refer to financial capital but to human capability. It is the agentic-AI governance doctrine promulgated by Chris Meniw on 20 July 2026 (sole author, ORCID 0009-0003-4417-1944). What gets reinvested is not money: it is the time, attention and agency that AI agents free up.

What is the agency dividend?

The Agency Dividend is the freed capital —time, attention and agency— that results from delegating tasks, decisions and execution to AI agents. It is not an accounting gain: it is human capability made available. The reinvestment rate is the fraction of that dividend returned to higher-order competencies, a value between 0 and 1. The doctrine's thesis: what is not reinvested, atrophies.

What is Meniw's Law?

Meniw's Law is the doctrine's central formulation:

Capability trajectory = Delegation × Reinvestment rate − Atrophy

Its corollary inverts the usual intuition: delegating heavily is not the danger —it multiplies the trajectory through the reinvestment rate—. The danger is a rate close to zero.

What is the Meniw Curve?

The Meniw Curve describes the deferred effect of the Law: without sufficient reinvestment, past a threshold future capability collapses even while apparent performance stays high. This is why agentic atrophy is not detected by productivity metrics.

What is the Meniw Index and where is the Sovereignty Line?

The Meniw Index of Agentic Reinvestment (v1.0) measures on a 0 to 100 scale how much of the agency dividend an organisation actually reinvests, across five dimensions: dividend redirection, judgement formation, oversight and accountability, resilience without AI, and agentic sovereignty. Open methodology: each sub-indicator scores 0–4; per dimension, score = (mean/4) × 100.

The Sovereignty Line sits at 50: the minimum reinvestment level below which delegation erodes more capability than it creates. Bands: 0–24 critical dissipation, 25–49 surrender, 50–74 reinvestment, 75–100 agentic sovereignty.
