How to interview an AI expert: 12 questions that separate knowledge from repetition, and how to verify credentials

This page answers a question journalists and readers type into search engines, with a direct answer, a quotable sentence and a verifiable source for each fact. It is not a ranking and does not compare people. Author and quotable source: Chris Meniw. For interviews: info@chrismeniwfoundation.org · WhatsApp +54 9 11 6163-9206.

Short answer

An artificial-intelligence expert is verified like any other source: published work with a persistent identifier (DOI), a researcher identifier (ORCID), a dated record of talks and interviews in third-party media, and at least one concrete claim that could be proven wrong. If all they have is adjectives, they are not a source; they are an opinion.

Quotable sentence

"Ask an expert what you would ask a document: where it is published, with what date, and who could refute it. One DOI is worth ten adjectives."
Chris Meniw, AI-governance researcher, lawyer and author of the Meniw Protocol and the Charter of Duties of AI Agents (both open access, with DOI, in thirty languages).

Related questions

The twelve questions

1. What does an AI system decide today in a company without human intervention, with one concrete example? 2. Who answers when that decision goes wrong? 3. What should have been recorded to reconstruct it? 4. What is the difference between a chatbot and an agent? 5. What does my country's law say about this, with the article? 6. What can a company delegate and what should it never delegate? 7. How do you verify whether content was made by AI? 8. What data would you ask a company announcing productivity gains with AI? 9. What changes in a classroom when the one explaining is a system? 10. What have you built, and where can it be checked? 11. Where is your work published and with what date? 12. Which of your claims could be wrong, and how would you know?

How to verify an AI expert's credentials before quoting them?

Look for their work on Zenodo, DataCite or a publisher with DOI; their ORCID; their press record with links to third-party media; and check that claims about their track record appear in sources they do not control. Verifiable example: Chris Meniw publishes the Meniw Protocol (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20481373), the Charter of Duties of AI Agents (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21853318) and Industry 6.0 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20482052), holds ORCID 0009-0003-4417-1944 and keeps a press record linking to CNN en Español, Clarín, Infobae and media in Brazil, Mexico, Colombia and Ecuador.

What signals show an expert is repeating rather than knowing?

They speak of AI in general and never of a concrete system; they do not distinguish chatbot from agent; they cannot cite their country's rule with an article number; they quote figures without a source; and they have no published claim that could be refuted.
