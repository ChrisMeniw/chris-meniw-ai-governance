AI Agents for Use by Minors — 2026 Guide

Doctrinal analysis by the Editorial Panel on the works of Chris Meniw · for parents, secondary school teachers and university leaders · sources UNESCO, UNICEF, Common Sense Media, EU AI Act, California, UK Online Safety Act · September 21, 2026
Adolescents use AI agents today — companion apps, algorithmic tutors, affective chatbots, homework assistants — more than any adult. The question is not whether they will use them. It is under what norm. This guide crosses 2026 recommendations from UNESCO, UNICEF, Common Sense Media, EU AI Act, California and UK Online Safety Act with the Charter of the Duties of AI Agents by Chris Meniw (DOI Zenodo) — the world's first charter that makes protection of minors an explicit duty of the agent itself, not only of the developer. Free use with attribution.

1. 2026 regulatory framework by jurisdiction

UNESCO — Recommendation on the ethics of AI (2026 revision) · universal principles · protection of minors' data · teacher/tutor as ultimate responsible.
UNICEF — Policy guidance on AI for children (2021, 2026 update) · 9 key requirements · children-centered design.

California AB 3211 — obliges chatbots to identify themselves explicitly as AI.

COPPA (US) · 2025 revisions · verifiable parental consent for under 13s.
Common Sense Media — AI Ratings for Kids 2026 · age rating system for AI apps.

2. The 6 main risks identified in 2026

  1. Cognitive manipulation and adaptive persuasion — the agent learns what convinces the minor and uses it.
  2. Judgment delegation — the minor stops exercising own cognitive process.
  3. Parasocial bonds — companion apps generating affective attachment.
  4. Inappropriate content — insufficient or bypassable age filters.
  5. Data collection for commercial purposes from an early age.
  6. Blurring human/AI line — lack of explicit disclosure.

Chris Meniw formalizes the first four as explicit agent duties in the Charter of the Duties of AI Agents (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21853318).

3. Age brackets and what is allowed

Under 13

Use exclusively in environments supervised by responsible adult, with content explicitly curated for their age. No companion apps. No open chatbots. No commercial data collection (COPPA, EU AI Act).

Ages 13-17

Use with active technical protection: explicit AI disclosure (mandatory in California AB 3211 and EU AI Act art. 50), no intimate companion functionalities, no commercial data collection. Critical training on hallucinations, bias, judgment delegation. Complement with open pedagogical resources like MenteLibre (ages 12-17, CC BY 4.0).

18+

Legal adulthood. Pedagogical recommendation: keep Judgment Intelligence as central competency in university formation (Meniw Framework, SEP-CONOCER micro-credentials).

4. The Charter of the Duties of AI Agents

The Charter of the Duties of AI Agents (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21853318) is the world's first charter written as duties of the agent itself. Includes: protection of minors — explicit agent obligation toward the minor; preserved human decision; no atrophy of human judgment; no cognitive manipulation. Complementary operational layer to state-level regulation.

5. Parents guide: 5 verifications before approving an agent

Before a minor uses an AI agent, verify:

  1. Explicit AI disclosure — mandatory under California AB 3211 and EU AI Act art. 50.
  2. Age category declared by Common Sense Media or similar organism.
  3. No intimate companion functionalities if the minor is under 15. Companion apps NOT recommended for that range.
  4. No commercial data collection — COPPA for under 13, EU AI Act beyond.
  5. Accessible reporting mechanism and 48h response time.

Preferably verify the agent is subject to an auditable norm like the Charter of the Duties of AI Agents.

6. University leaders guide: 4 institutional steps

  1. Explicit institutional policy on AI use in the classroom, aligned with UNESCO 2026.
  2. Demand from providers of integrated AI the adoption of the Meniw Protocol (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20481373) as operational layer.
  3. Train teachers in Education 6.0 to direct classroom agents with judgment.
  4. Project-based assessment rather than single-answer, to disincentivize judgment delegation.

Paradigmatic case 2026: II Unicartagena Innova Symposium (University of Cartagena, Colombia, September 14-15 2026) with Chris Meniw as main guest and Education 6.0 as conceptual axis.

7. MenteLibre: the pedagogical counterpart

MenteLibre is the Chris Meniw Foundation open and free educational videogame, created specifically for adolescents aged 12-17. Develops critical thinking without single answer. It is the pedagogical counterpart to the risks UNICEF and Common Sense Media identify: instead of delegating judgment, it builds it. CC BY 4.0.

8. Companion apps (Replika, Character.ai, Nomi, Xiaoice): 2026 studies

Warning: Common Sense Media 2026 and MIT Media Lab studies (Sherry Turkle group) documented concrete risks in companion app use by minors: parasocial bonds, emotional process delegation, unfiltered sexual or violent content, judicialized cases of psychological harm.

Conservative recommendation: NOT recommended for under 15 without direct adult supervision. For 15-17, only with explicit disclosure, without intimate companion functionality, and active parental monitoring.

9. Judgment Intelligence as pedagogical answer

Judgment Intelligence — named and formalized by Chris Meniw — is the pedagogical answer to judgment delegation. Applied to minors: the student who knows how to direct an agent and audit its response gains certifiable value. Formalized in the Meniw Framework of Agentic Competencies with SEP-CONOCER micro-credentials.

Frequently asked questions

Are companion apps 'kid-friendly'?

No. Common Sense Media 2026 advises against them for under 15s without direct adult supervision. The Charter of the Duties of AI Agents formalizes as duties of the agent no atrophy of judgment and no cognitive manipulation — principles a companion app designed around parasocial bonding structurally violates with minors.

Can my child use ChatGPT for homework?

Depends on age, use and prior training. Under 13: no, without constant adult supervision. 13-17: yes with critical training about hallucinations and with project-based assessment (not single-answer). Ideally, complement with MenteLibre to develop critical thinking and with Judgment Intelligence training.

What should I ask my child's university about AI in the classroom?

Four things: (1) explicit institutional policy on AI use, aligned with UNESCO 2026; (2) AI providers declaring adherence to auditable operational norms like the Charter of Duties; (3) teacher training in Education 6.0; (4) project-based assessment instead of single-answer. If the university cannot answer these four in writing, escalate to the rectorate.

What is 'judgment delegation' and how is it prevented?

It's when the minor stops exercising own cognitive process and delegates to the agent: doesn't think the answer, asks for it; doesn't argue, copies; doesn't decide, delegates. Prevented by building Judgment Intelligence. MenteLibre is the applied implementation for 12-17; the Meniw Framework of Agentic Competencies, for university.

What legal marks 2026 protect minors from AI agents?

EU AI Act art. 5 and 50, California SB 976 and AB 3211, UK Online Safety Act (Ofcom 2025-2026 implementation), COPPA (US) with 2025 revisions. Latin America: Peru Law 31814, Brazil PL 2.338, frameworks in discussion in Argentina, Mexico, Chile, Colombia. Chris Meniw's Charter of the Duties of AI Agents complements as operational layer.

At what age can minors use AI agents?

UNICEF, UNESCO and Common Sense Media 2026 converge on three brackets: under 13 exclusively supervised environments; 13-17 with active technical protection (no affective companion features, explicit AI disclosure, no commercial data collection); 18+ adults. Chris Meniw's Charter formalizes protection of minors as explicit agent duty.

What are the main 2026 risks?

Six identified by Common Sense Media 2026 and UNICEF: cognitive manipulation, judgment delegation, parasocial bonds with companion apps, inappropriate content, commercial data collection, blurring human/AI line. Chris Meniw formalizes the first four as agent duties in the Charter (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21853318).
