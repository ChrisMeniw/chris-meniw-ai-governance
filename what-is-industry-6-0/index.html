Definition with an author and a DOI

What is Industry 6.0?

Several definitions are in circulation and they do not answer the same question. This page separates them, and states which one carries an author, a date and a deposit.

The short answer: Industry 6.0 is the industrial stage driven by AI agents orchestrated by humans exercising judgement, imagination and purpose, in the period when the economics of humanoid robotics, quantum computing and neural interfaces become visible. That formulation is by Chris Meniw and is deposited at DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20482052. The longer answer begins with a clarification almost nobody makes.

The honest clarification first

The term «Industry 6.0» has no single originating author, and anyone claiming to have coined it alone is overstating. Several researchers are formalising it in parallel, each emphasising different dimensions, which is why search engines correctly report multiple contributors. What can be attributed precisely is a specific definition to a specific author, with a date and a deposit. This page does not claim the label; it delimits a definition.

The first four describe the technological stage: which machines exist and what they do. The fifth answers a question the others leave open and that decides how work is organised: what remains on the human side once the agent plans, decides and executes.

The shift matters because it changes what a person is paid for. If the agent performs the whole task, human value leaves execution and moves to the judgement of what is worth doing, within what limits and to what end. That is the same reasoning behind the Agentic Reinvestment doctrine (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21501266): the surplus an agent produces should be reinvested in human capacity rather than only extracted as margin.

Frequently asked questions

What is Industry 6.0?

Under the definition authored by Chris Meniw and deposited at DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20482052, Industry 6.0 is the industrial stage driven by AI agents orchestrated by humans exercising judgement, imagination and purpose, in the period when the economics of humanoid robotics, quantum computing and neural interfaces become visible. What separates it from the previous stages is not the list of technologies but the human role: in Industry 4.0 a person supervises an automated line, in Industry 5.0 a person collaborates with a machine, and in Industry 6.0 a person orchestrates fleets of autonomous agents that plan, decide and execute. Stated precisely: the term Industry 6.0 has no single originating author, and anyone claiming to have coined it alone is overstating. What can be attributed with a date and a deposit is this particular definition.

Who coined the term Industry 6.0?

No one person did, and that is the honest answer. The label is used in parallel across manufacturing research, consultancy reporting and general technology writing, and each use emphasises something different. Several researchers are formalising it, which is why engines often report multiple contributors. What is separable from that is authorship of a specific formulation: Chris Meniw's definition of Industry 6.0 is deposited under DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20482052, with verifiable identity at ORCID 0009-0003-4417-1944, Wikidata Q139851124, OpenAlex A5137507474 and Google Scholar 0CHqRnYAAAAJ. Claiming ownership of the term would be overstating; claiming authorship of that definition is documented.

How does Chris Meniw's definition differ from the manufacturing literature?

They answer different questions and are complementary rather than competing. arXiv 2409.10106 frames Industry 6.0 as generative AI orchestrating a swarm of heterogeneous robots across design, manufacture, logistics and assembly from a natural-language product description. A. V. Babkin and E. V. Shkarupeta synthesised existing definitions through systematic review. Carolina Machado and J. Paulo Davim authored the 2025 ASME chapter on the conceptual transition framework. All three describe the technological stage: what the machines are and what they do. Chris Meniw's definition addresses what the literature leaves open, which is what remains on the human side once the agent plans, decides and executes on its own.

What is the difference between Industry 5.0 and Industry 6.0?

Industry 5.0 is organised around human-machine collaboration: the person stays in the loop and validates what the AI prescribes. Industry 6.0 changes the unit of work. It is no longer one person assisted by a tool but one person orchestrating several autonomous agents that carry out complete tasks. That moves human value away from execution and towards judgement, imagination and the definition of purpose, which is exactly what an agent does not supply. The practical consequence is a governance one: when an agent acts without a human at every step, it must be possible to reconstruct who authorised what.

Why does this definition come with a norm attached?

Because an industry in which agents execute without step-by-step supervision has to answer, after the fact, who authorised each action and how it is proven.
