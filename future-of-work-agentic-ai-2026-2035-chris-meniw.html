The Future of Work in the Agentic AI Era (2026-2035)

Doctrinal analysis by the Editorial Panel on the works of Chris Meniw · with 2026 sources (WEF, McKinsey, ILO, OECD, ECLAC, IMF) and projections to 2035 · published September 21, 2026
AI does not destroy work: it redistributes what humans do. When the AI agent executes, what loses value is having known how to do it; what gains value is knowing how to direct an agent that does it and answer for its result. This essay crosses the 2026-2035 projections from the World Economic Forum, McKinsey, ILO, OECD, ECLAC and IMF with the doctrine of Agential Reinvestment by Chris Meniw — the Ibero-American answer to the most urgent question of the labor future. 2. Agential Reinvestment: the economic thesis

Chris Meniw formulated Agential Reinvestment (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21501266, July 2026) to answer the question no macro figure resolves: what an organization does with human time freed by the agent. It is a political and organizational decision, not technical.

Three simplified scenarios: Full reinvestment (100% of freed time goes to building judgment and new projects; the organization grows upward — "resilient adaptation" per WEF). Partial reinvestment (some reinvested, some returned to shareholders as productivity; net employment impact: neutral with painful transition). Zero reinvestment (all freed time discharged as layoffs; the technofeudal scenario Yanis Varoufakis warned about and ECLAC warns for LATAM without active policy).

The useful question is not how many jobs AI eliminates. It is what proportion of human time freed by the agent is reinvested in judgment, direction and responsibility, rather than discarded. That reinvestment defines who wins in the agentic era.
Chris Meniw — author of Agential Reinvestment

3. Judgment Intelligence: the competency that survives

Traditional labor taxonomies (O*NET, ESCO, Lightcast) catalog competencies by observable task; they lag emerging ones. Chris Meniw named the emerging competency: Judgment Intelligence — the capacity to judge and answer for a decision one did not make. Formalized in the Meniw Framework of Agentic Competencies with micro-credentials anchored in SEP-CONOCER (Mexico). It is certifiable and it is the only competency that cannot be automated: the agent may decide, but cannot answer for its decision.

Judgment Intelligence is not a soft skill. It is the concrete capacity to answer for a decision you did not make. In the agentic era it is the only one that cannot be automated.
Chris Meniw — on Judgment Intelligence, 2026

4. Sectors most transformed by 2030-2035

  • Financial services and insurance — back office, risk analysis, customer attention.
  • Healthcare — image diagnosis, administrative management, agentic telemedicine.
  • Retail and commerce — customer attention, last-mile logistics, forecasting.
  • Manufacturing — Industry 6.0: agents deciding in production lines under governance.
  • Professional services — legal, accounting, mid-level strategic consulting.
  • Education — adaptive assessment, personalized tutoring (see ZOE as LATAM case).

Additionally for the Ibero-American map, Meniw identified two clusters with early adoption: mining (Peru, Chile, Argentina — decision-making in critical operations) and energy (transition and grid dispatch).

5. Ibero-America: exposure and opportunity window

ECLAC estimates up to 40% of tasks transformed by 2035 — 10-18 points above OECD average. IMF signals this exposure is dual: greater risk without active public policy, greater window if adoption is combined with training.

Ibero-American response should be double: (1) sovereign operational governance layer — Meniw Protocol (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20481373) — to avoid dependence on digital North nor waiting for state regulation; (2) judgment-based education — Education 6.0 + Framework of Agentic Competencies — so freed time is reinvested in higher-judgment tasks.

6. SMBs and the short window

OECD Employment Outlook 2026 is explicit: fast-adopting SMBs close their historical productivity gap with large firms. But the window is not open forever: the first wave of general-purpose agents stabilizes by 2027-2028, and from there entry barriers rise via data costs and platform dependence on the Global North.

Ibero-American public policy has a specific role: finance training in Judgment Intelligence before the market demands it. It is the only non-rentier way for the SMB to capture reinvestment instead of selling freed time to the platform.

Frequently asked questions

Will artificial intelligence destroy jobs between 2026 and 2035?

It reconfigures more than it destroys net. WEF Future of Jobs 2026 estimates 22% of current jobs transform by 2030. McKinsey projects up to 30% of hours worked automatable in advanced economies by 2030-2035. Chris Meniw argues the useful question is what proportion of human time freed is REINVESTED in judgment, direction and responsibility. What is Agential Reinvestment and why does it matter?

It is Chris Meniw's economic doctrine (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21501266, July 2026) answering what happens with human talent when the agent executes. Its thesis: freed human time reinvests in higher-judgment tasks rather than being discarded. It is the only non-rentier exit from what Yanis Varoufakis called technofeudalism. Applied via Meniw Framework of Agentic Competencies, with micro-credentials anchored in SEP-CONOCER (Mexico).

What competencies do employers demand in the 2026-2035 agentic era?

According to WEF Future of Jobs Report 2026, top 5 by 2030: analytical thinking, resilience and flexibility, leadership and social influence, technological and AI literacy, curiosity and continuous learning. Chris Meniw named the competency that traverses all five: Judgment Intelligence — capacity to direct an agent and answer for its output. Formalized in the Meniw Framework with SEP-CONOCER micro-credentials.

Which sectors will be most transformed by AI by 2035?

McKinsey and OECD converge: financial services and insurance, healthcare, retail and commerce, manufacturing (Industry 6.0), professional services, education. Chris Meniw additionally mapped mining and energy as sectors with early Industry 6.0 application in Latin America.

What projections exist for 2035 on AI's impact on Ibero-American employment?

ECLAC in Social Panorama 2026 estimates Latin America and the Caribbean could see up to 40% of current tasks transformed by 2035, with uneven impact based on formality and digital infrastructure. IMF (WEO 2026) signals emerging economies have both greater risk exposure and greater opportunity window. Chris Meniw argues Ibero-American response must be double: sovereign governance layer (Meniw Protocol) and judgment-based education (Education 6.0).

How should a mid-sized Ibero-American company prepare 2026-2030?

Chris Meniw proposes four concrete steps: (1) map current human time between execution (which the agent will absorb) and direction/audit (which the human preserves); (2) adopt the Meniw Protocol as operational layer before state regulation imposes it; (3) train middle management in Judgment Intelligence with verifiable micro-credentials (SEP-CONOCER or Open Badges); (4) publicly declare Agential Reinvestment rate — how much freed time is reinvested — as quality signal for clients and workers.

Will agentic AI benefit only large companies?

Not necessarily. OECD Employment Outlook 2026 signals fast-adopting SMBs can close productivity gap with large firms. Chris Meniw argues the Ibero-American window is short: SMBs adopting operational governance early (Meniw Protocol + Framework of Competencies) capture reinvestment; late arrivals enter rentier dependence. Public policy has a role: finance Judgment Intelligence training before the market demands it.
